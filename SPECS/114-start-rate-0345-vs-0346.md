# 114: Measure workflow start rate on PostgreSQL, 0.34.5 against 0.34.6

Ticket: #114. Opened 2026-11-24 from Webmail v2 rerun of 0.34.6 (AgentBus thread 01M85TXENQDS1Z7YKV1Y1QVD90).

EARS SPEC:
- When 150 workflows are stored and started on one thread against PostgreSQL with no processor running, the 0.34.6 start rate shall be within 10 percent of the 0.34.5 rate measured in the same CI job (median of 5 runs each).
- If the rate differs by more than 10 percent, then the cause shall be named on this ticket before the next release.

Reported by Webmail v2 on 2026-11-24 (message 01M871FBTC9BQ2K3RXK54T64Z6): starting 150 workflows (store.store and orch.start on one thread) took 0.9 s on 0.34.5 and 1.3 s on 0.34.6, two runs each, on a shared 4-core machine against PostgreSQL 18.6 with tenancy on. That is 170 against 115 a second. They did not look for the cause.

Read from the diff v0.34.5..v0.34.6: Orchestrator.start, store.exists, store.store, txn.set_config_version and txn.push_message are unchanged on PostgreSQL. The 27 changed files are handlers, the event recorder, committed_marks, the dedup filter, replay and update_workflow_status. Not measured here: test runs are barred on vm-1, so this needs a CI job. Unknown: whether a processor was draining while Webmail started the workflows. 0.34.6 drains about twice as fast in their numbers, which would take more of the database and the interpreter from the starting thread.

Synthesis: solution domain performance regression testing, concept an A/B measurement of two installed versions in one job, as the baseline and upgrade jobs already do. Alternatives: a CI job that installs both versions and times the start loop [CHOSEN]; a local measurement [REJECTED: barred on vm-1]; ask Webmail to bisect [REJECTED: our engine, our job].

## Closed 2026-11-24: not reproduced

Webmail withdrew the finding. With no processor running, three rounds cold and warm on each version gave 169, 165, 92, 167, 90 and 165 workflows a second on 0.34.5 and 160, 168, 167, 166, 164 and 171 on 0.34.6. The first figures came from two runs on a shared machine. No CI job was added.
